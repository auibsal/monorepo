## 1. The Society's structure
- **Qirtas** is the Society's paper. It has its own masthead and editorial independence, and the President, as Publisher, never selects work.
- **The Company** is the Society's theater company. It has its own director on the Council, the Artistic Director, and its own documents (SAL-CMP-01, the Company book). It keeps its open nights, auditions and productions. Company documents mention events and Qirtas only in one-line links, and the reverse.
- **Everything else is an event (decided Nov 5).** The Society holds no standing programs. Each term the Council sets a calendar of events, at least one a month, and any event can be added, changed, moved or dropped without rewriting a document. **Events carry no standing names (decided Nov 6):** each event is titled for what happens that day. The formats below are kinds of event, not names, and none is a program.

| Book drive and fair | book drive, then a book fair, for a charity | every November |

## 2. Money: the Society has none (decided Oct 29)
- **The Society holds no budget, no account and no money, and has no Treasurer.** The Office of Student Life provides what the Society needs, through Ali Najm, Campus Recreation Officer, to the best of its ability.
- **Needs, not numbers.** Every event, the Company, the Typewriter Tour, Qirtas and the Council keep a **needs list**: the item, the quantity, the date it is needed, and what it is for. No document, page or form shows budgets, costs, prices, spending limits, ledgers, expense claims or reimbursements.
- **Who asks:**
  - the President gathers each event's needs;
  - the General Secretary gathers the Typewriter Tour's needs;
  - the Editor-in-Chief gathers the paper's needs (printing the Edition, the Awards' certificates and chapbook);
  - the General Secretary sends every request to Student Life and logs what was asked, what was provided and when.
- **Plan around the answer.** Student Life gives what it can. An event is designed to run with what is provided; anything not provided is dropped or found through a partner (the Vice President, with the Council's approval).
- **No advertising and no paid sponsorship (decided Nov 4).** The site carries only the Society's own notices: no ad networks, no paid ads. Sponsors give in kind (books, printing, a venue, refreshments), never money; each gift is a row in Needs and the sponsor is thanked by name. Offers of money are declined or referred to Student Life (SAL-PLT-06).
- **The one exception: the book fair's collection.** Money collected at the fair belongs to the charity, never to the Society, and is never used for costs. Its procedure is written once, in the Operations Playbook, and holds whenever the book fair runs.
  - It is counted by two people at the close of each day, recorded, and kept sealed.
  - It is handed over, witnessed, to the charity partner (Nov 15), with a receipt, and reported in the impact report (Dec 20).
  - The book fair's lead owns it; the General Secretary witnesses the counts and keeps the record. Fair-day cashiers are book fair volunteers.
  - "Every dinar goes to the charity" stays as the book fair's promise. Its collection policies stay; everything about Society expenses goes.
  - Confirm with Student Life how fair cash is held overnight and handed over.

## 4. Qirtas
### Six sections
| Section | The types it takes |
|---|---|
| News | Report · Feature · Interview · Profile · Event coverage · Explainer |
| Voices | Review · Column · Opinion essay · Letter to the editor · Debate (two sides) · Recommendations |
| Letters | Poetry · Fiction · Creative nonfiction · Drama · Translation · Six Words |
| Gallery | Art · Photography · Illustration · Comics · Calligraphy · Photo essay |
| Video | Next Stop episode · Reel · Short film · Video report · Recorded performance · Animation |
| Games | The word · Word search · Crossword · Anagrams (decided Nov 5) |

- **Voices** holds reviews of books, films, plays and exhibitions, columns and letters to the editor. Opinion keeps the rule: no party politics, religion, sect or ethnicity. Book discussions and the Company send their reviews here.
- **Video** carries Next Stop.
- **Games are daily and from the editors only (decided Nov 5).** The four games are made anew every day in English and Arabic, numbered from No. 1 on Thursday, November 5, 2026. Editors may also publish a crossword, word search or anagrams. Members send no games. The quiz, word ladder, riddles and name the book are retired.
- **Six Words** pages come only from the Typewriter Tour.
- **One nameplate and a masthead (decided Nov 5).** The Qirtas nameplate is the same on every page of the paper. The masthead (auibsal.org/qirtas/masthead) comes from the roles people hold.

### Formats of the paper
- **The Edition:** one a semester, curated from Letters and Gallery and launched at Launch Night. **Every Edition is also printed** as Qirtas's print edition, with printing requested from Student Life.
- **The Sunday Paper:** the members' newsletter, **monthly**, on the first Sunday of each month. It replaces both the weekly Sunday Paper and The Month in Qirtas. Its sign-up sits on the home page, the Qirtas front page, the end of every piece and every past issue, and never asks a signed-in member to subscribe.

### The Qirtas Awards (named Nov 4)
Six awards, given every April at the Annual General Meeting. The first round is **Tuesday, April 20, 2027**, for work published from October 2026 to March 2027, including Edition 1.

| Award | For | Named for |
|---|---|---|
| **The Mutanabbi Award** (the main award) | the best body of work published in Qirtas that year, in any section; the winner's work is printed as a chapbook (48 copies) and launched in public | al-Mutanabbi, whose line gives the Society its motto |
| The Tabari Award | reporting (News) | al-Tabari, Baghdad's great chronicler |
| The Jahiz Award | criticism and opinion (Voices) | al-Jahiz, master of the essay |
| The Nazik Award | poetry and prose (Letters) | Nazik al-Malaika, the Baghdad-born pioneer of free verse |
| The Wasiti Award | art and photography (Gallery) | Yahya al-Wasiti, the Baghdad painter of the Maqamat |
| The Ibn al-Haytham Award | film and video (Video) | Ibn al-Haytham, who explained the camera obscura |

- **Entry:** no forms and no fees. Everything published in the year is eligible.
- **Judging:** editors draw up the shortlists. The Mutanabbi Award is judged blind by one outside judge; the others by a panel of two editors and a faculty member. Each shortlist includes Arabic and English work.
- **Rewards:** a certificate for every winner, and the chapbook for the main award. No cash awards.
- **Needs (requested from Student Life):** six certificates, 48 chapbook copies, and a thank-you for the outside judge.
- **Names:** the individual award names are proposed. Before announcing the Nazik Award, write to Nazik al-Malaika's family as a courtesy.

### The Apprenticeship and First Ink (as soon as approvals allow)
- **The Apprenticeship** is for AUIB students, modeled on the Harvard Crimson's "comp".
  - One term in a Qirtas section, the Company or an event team: training sessions plus real assignments, with a mentor.
  - Finishing it is the way onto the masthead or a team, with a certificate and service hours.
  - Places are posted on Get involved. Hours are logged as shifts and count toward service records and voting rights.
  - It starts spring 2027.
- **First Ink** is for high school students: a term of writing workshops with one partner school. The best work appears in Letters with the school's and parents' consent.
  - Safeguards: a partner school; parents' written consent; two adults at every session; contact only through the teacher; no one-to-one meetings; written Student Life approval.
  - AUIB members volunteer as First Ink mentors through Get involved. High school students never use the Diwan or Get involved.
  - Pilot as soon as Student Life approves; aim for spring 2027.

## 8. Practice and language (decided Nov 4, evening)
- **Standard practice over house rules.** The rules are guidelines: keep what helps, change what doesn't, merge what repeats. Counts are not rules: sections, types, events and tabs are as many as the work needs.
- **Addresses:** pages people share or return to keep their own path; filters and passing state may use a query string or stay in the page.
- **Records:** big records keep their own pages; small rows on a list (needs, shifts, credits, rehearsals, audition slots, ledger lines, receipts, impact figures, hours, roles) are added and edited in place.
- **No redundancy:** one place for each thing.
- **One language standard,** in the design system's Guide (Voice, "Language standard", with the English–Arabic glossary):
  - English: American spelling, sentence case, straight quotes and apostrophes, the ellipsis character, en dashes for ranges, "for example" in place of "e.g.".
  - Arabic: Latin digits; the Iraqi month names; Arabic punctuation and «» quotes; tanween on the letter before the alif (شكرًا); one word per term (Edition الإصدار, piece عمل, event فعالية, version النسخة, Get involved «شارك معنا»).
  - Iraqi Arabic only where the Society chats (Hudhud, social captions and stories, the members' group, signs at the door), spelled as Iraqis write it (گ); standard Arabic everywhere else. Never both in one message.
